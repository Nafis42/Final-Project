Project Overview & Research Framework

Research questions, institutional context, and empirical dataset architecture.

The Indian Petroleum Supply-Chain Paradox: Over the past 26 fiscal years, India transformed into one of the world's leading refining powerhouses and a structural net exporter of transport fuels (High Speed Diesel, Petrol, Jet Fuel), while simultaneously experiencing a dramatic escalation in raw crude oil import dependence from 54.86% to 88.86%, alongside an acute structural deficit in household cooking gas (LPG).

Core Research Questions (RQ1 - RQ5)

  • RQ1: Domestic Production vs. Imports: Has domestic upstream extraction stagnated, and does domestic extraction exert any measurable crowding-out effect on physical crude imports?
  • RQ2: Downstream Refining as Import Driver: How much of the crude import surge is mathematically attributable to the 282% expansion in domestic refining throughput?
  • RQ3: Product Trade Bifurcation: How does India reconcile macroscopic refined product self-sufficiency (>117%) with a massive -17.99 MMT structural import deficit in domestic LPG?
  • RQ4: Financial Bill Volatility: What fraction of the 38-fold increase in import expenditure is driven by global price shocks vs. physical volumes, and how severe is the Rupee depreciation wedge?
  • RQ5: Structural Transitions & Policies: How have landmark welfare policies (PAHAL, PMUY) displaced traditional fuels (Kerosene) and altered domestic consumption compositions?

Econometric Investigation: The Crowding-Out Myth

A common assumption in energy debates is that domestic extraction displaces crude imports. Our empirical evaluation conclusively refutes this: bivariate correlation between domestic crude extraction and crude imports is zero ($r = 0.096, p = 0.641$). In annual first differences, $r = 0.173, p = 0.408$. In multiple regression controlling for refinery demand, the crowding-out elasticity of domestic production on imports is statistically indistinguishable from zero ($\beta = -0.3204, t = -1.13, p = 0.2713$).
